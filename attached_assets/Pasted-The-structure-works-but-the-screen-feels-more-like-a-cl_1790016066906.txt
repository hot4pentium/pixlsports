The structure works, but the screen feels more like a clean productivity app than an energetic sports experience. I would keep the same information architecture and modernize the presentation.
Recommended direction: “Game Week Energy”
Use the new PIXL Sports blue as the primary color, deep navy for contrast, and a small amount of orange for urgency.
Electric blue: #2878F5
Deep navy: #0B1F3A
Bright blue: #55A4FF
Game-day orange: #FF6433
Success green: #16B981
Cool page background: #F3F6FA
Biggest improvements
Turn the athlete area into a compact hero
The large empty cream section should become a navy or blue-gradient athlete banner.
Enlarge the athlete photo to approximately 72–84 px.
Show “RANDAL” prominently.
Put #30 • TIGERS beneath it.
Add a subtle stadium-light, field-line, or halftone texture.
Place “Good afternoon, Brian” above it in smaller text instead of making it the dominant headline.
Make the next event the visual centerpiece
Replace the plain white calendar card with a more energetic game card:
Dark navy-to-blue gradient.
Large date block on the left: SEP / 18.
TIGERS vs WOLVES in white.
Orange GAME DAY or UP NEXT pill.
Location and time underneath.
Very faint field markings or stadium lighting in the background.
Add a small countdown such as 2 DAYS AWAY.
Modernize the four-step journey
The current line with four circles feels dated and takes up too much horizontal space.
Use four connected compact tabs:
PLAN ✓ PREP ✓ CAPTURE ✓ SEND 4
Completed stages can use blue checks instead of large green circles. The active stage should become a filled blue pill with a soft glow or animated pulse. Keep orange only for a small NOW marker.
Give “Your Next Best Step” more energy
This should feel like an action card, not another white information panel.
Add a blue gradient strip along the top or left.
Include a small lightning-bolt or celebration icon.
Use a relevant thumbnail from the athlete or game.
Make the action clear: CREATE A HYPE CARD.
Use a bold blue button with a subtle diagonal cut or arrow.
Add a secondary option such as View recent moments.
Use subtle motion and texture
Modern sports energy does not require a busy interface.
Slowly shift the gradient in the next-game card.
Pulse the active journey step once when the page loads.
Slide the recommended action upward by 10–15 px.
Add faint dots, diagonal lines, or field markings at 3–5% opacity.
Use soft blue shadows rather than gray shadows.
Clean up the header
The logo is currently clipped and the email address makes the header feel administrative.
Display the complete logo with more breathing room.
Replace the visible email with a circular profile button.
Put Help, Privacy, and Sign Out inside the profile menu.
On mobile, use only the logo and profile/avatar.
Suggested mobile hierarchy
Compact header
Athlete hero
Next-game hero card
Four-stage journey
Recommended action
Recent designs or quick tools
Bottom navigation
The goal is for the page to immediately communicate:
Here is your athlete. Here is what is happening next. Here is what you should do now.
That would make the dashboard feel current, sports-focused, and energetic without returning to the heavy industrial style you were trying to move away from.
