## PRODUCT POSITIONING

Pixl Sports is a modern youth-sports platform for parents, supporters, coaches, and athletes.

Pixl does more than provide individual tools. It guides visitors through the sports week—from planning and preparation to game day and celebration—so families can spend less time chasing information and more time supporting their athletes.

The central brand promise is:

“We guide your week. You capture the moments.”

The supporting brand statement is:

“Less chasing. More cheering.”

The landing page should communicate this promise clearly and emotionally within the first screen.

## TARGET AUDIENCE

Design primarily for parents and supporters.

Coaches may use Pixl Sports, but the landing page should not feel like coaching software, league administration software, or a technical operations dashboard.

A parent visiting the page should immediately understand:

* Pixl helps organize the sports week.
* Pixl makes game day easier to follow.
* Pixl helps preserve and share meaningful moments.
* The platform is simple and welcoming.
* The tools are made for people supporting an athlete.

## OVERALL DESIGN DIRECTION

Create a modern sports-editorial experience.

The site should feel:

* Energetic
* Emotional
* Clean
* Bright
* Approachable
* Family-focused
* Sports-centered
* Premium but not corporate
* Modern but not futuristic

Use large sports photography, generous white space, bold editorial typography, clear calls to action, and polished product previews.

The photography should provide most of the sports energy. Avoid filling the interface with generic sports decorations.

## REMOVE THE CURRENT INDUSTRIAL STYLE

Do not reuse the current visual language.

Avoid:

* Dark navy covering the entire page
* Technical grids
* Fine background lines
* HUD or control-room styling
* Labels such as “LIVE STUDIO,” “HOME BASE,” or “SELECTED TOOL”
* Excessive uppercase copy
* Small technical labels
* Numerous outlined panels
* Dashboard information inside the main hero
* Neon-green esports styling
* Metallic effects
* Hexagonal decorations
* Stadium-light effects
* Shields or generic team crests
* Dense, compartmentalized layouts
* Excessive cards
* Excessive borders
* Futuristic or industrial textures

This must feel like a genuine change in the site’s visual language, not merely a rearrangement of the existing components.

## COLOR PALETTE

Use this palette:

* Warm off-white background: #F7F5EF
* Deep navy typography: #12213A
* Athletic blue primary accent: #2474F5
* Warm orange secondary accent: #FF6B35
* Pale blue supporting background: #E8F1FF

* Soft neutral gray for secondary text and dividers

Use athletic blue for primary actions and important emphasis.

Use warm orange sparingly for personality, handwritten accents, small highlights, or directional details.

Do not use neon green as a dominant landing-page color.

## TYPOGRAPHY

Use a bold, slightly condensed sports-editorial typeface for major headings.

Use a clean, highly readable sans-serif typeface for body text, navigation, buttons, and supporting content.

Typography should feel athletic but not like an esports logo or industrial poster.

Use sentence case for most interface copy. Reserve uppercase text for short eyebrow labels only.

Headlines should be bold and expressive, while body copy should remain friendly and easy to read.
